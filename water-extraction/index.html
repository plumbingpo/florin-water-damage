How to Prevent Water Extraction Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Worn or aging hoses on washers, dishwashers and ice makers should be replaced before they fail
  • Critical areas of the house should each have a leak sensor that you know is working
  • Find the main shutoff valve for the house and put a clear label on it
  • Keep valuables and stored boxes off the basement floor on shelves or pallets
  • Early leak signs show up near appliances, toilets and sink cabinets, so check those spots on a schedule

Why Water Extraction Is Not a DIY Job

The speed and thoroughness of water extraction directly determine the extent of damage to your property and the cost of restoration. Professional truck-mounted extraction equipment removes water hundreds of times faster than consumer tools, so what might take you an entire day with a shop vacuum takes our equipment minutes. Hidden moisture, the kind consumer tools never reach, is part of a professional extraction too. This thorough approach reduces drying time, lowers equipment costs and, most importantly, prevents the hidden mold growth that results from trapped moisture.

How to Tell You Need Water Extraction

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Visible water where your furnace, water heater or crawl space access is
  • Seams in the floor that ooze water under foot pressure
  • Pooled water on the carpet, or a soggy feel with every step
  • Wet baseboards and water creeping from beneath kitchen or bath cabinets
  • Water entering through a flooded basement stairwell or window well
  • Water sitting on a floor anywhere in the building, no matter where it came from
  • A basement taking on water after heavy rain or a sump pump failure
